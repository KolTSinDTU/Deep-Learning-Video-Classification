\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What the comparison isolates.}
Because every variant shares the backbone, recipe, update budget, and
initial per-frame weights, differences in \cref{tab:fusion} reflect where
frames are combined, not which network a model received. Under this control, where an
RGB network fuses time barely matters: the variants are within noise on the
leaky splits, and on the corrected splits they stay within 7\,pp of each
other on test (9\,pp on validation), with the two splits disagreeing about
their order. From 500
videos, the RGB networks did not learn motion features that reliably
generalise to new people.

\paragraph{Appearance shortcuts.}
A drop of 49--57\,pp for every RGB model, with near-perfect training
accuracy, means that on the leaky splits the task largely reduces to
recognising people and settings. Leaky evaluation therefore overstates
accuracy by more than a factor of two, and a model chosen on leaky splits
says little about how it will perform on new people.

\paragraph{Motion.}
Optical flow removes most appearance cues by construction, which is why the
flow stream overfits more slowly and generalises best. RGB still adds
complementary evidence: the streams fail on different classes, so combining
them helps whether the streams are fused at the score level or trained
jointly.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results use one seed and 120 videos per evaluation split; one video is
0.83\,pp, and the binomial standard error near 50\% accuracy is about
4.6\,pp. Validation accuracies are also optimistic, because the checkpoint
is the best of 60 noisy epochs: on the corrected splits, the RGB models
average only 28--37\% over their last ten epochs. Colour-coded flow is a lossy encoding of
the displacement vectors and rules out flip augmentation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We compared four ways of fusing time in a shared 2D/3D backbone, measured
the effect of person leakage, and added an optical-flow stream. On the
leaky splits all fusion points perform equally well (91--93\% test
accuracy). Person-disjoint splits reveal that this performance came mostly
from recognising people: every RGB model loses 49--57\,pp of test
accuracy, and the variants become indistinguishable at 35--42\%. Motion is
the cue that transfers: a flow stream on nine stacked RAFT fields reaches
49.2\% test accuracy, 7.5\,pp above the best RGB model, and fusing it with
the RGB stream reaches 54.2\%, 15.8\,pp above RGB alone.
The main limitation is overfitting on 500 training videos; pretraining the
RGB stream and averaging over seeds are the natural next steps.
